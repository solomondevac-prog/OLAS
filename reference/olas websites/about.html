OLAS BRIEF HISTORY

In the Academic Year 2010-2011, St. Francis School-Quezon City, through the assis- tance of St. Francis School-Sta. Ana, began to offer programs for Nursery and Kinder within the compound of the Our Lady of the Angels Seminary. The following Academic Year 2011-2012, St. Francis School- Quezon City started offering program for Grade I. Then St. Francis School-Quezon City began offering Grades II, III, IV, V and VI for the succeeding Academic Years. The groundbreaking of the K to 12 Building held on Octo- ber 7, 2016. In 2017, Saint Francis School-Quezon City began to be integrated to OLAS-College; it was renamed OLAS-Basic Education Department or OLAS-BED.

In Academic Year 2018-2019, the new school building, located at the back of Rieti (Rockville gate) was utilized for the first time. In February 18, 2019, Government recog- nition was granted to Kindergarten and Elementary courses, effective AY 2019-2020. On the same year, Government recognition was granted to Junior High School and Senior High School permit for Academic strands Accountancy and Business Manage- ment (ABM) and Humanities and Social Sciences (HUMSS) effective AY 2020-2021.

DepEd Order No. 17 s. 2022 (Guidelines on the Progressive Expansion of Face-to-face classes) was issued in April of AY 2022-2023. OLAS-BED complies using the Blended Learning Modality (BLR2) and the Fully Online Modality (BLR2) to accommodate en- rolled learners outside Novaliches area. PEAC granted accreditation to OLAS-BED on April 2023, ESC subsidy and SHS-Voucher was offered to Grades 7-10 and Grade 11 students the following school year.

In AY 2023-2024, OLAS-BED returns to the five-day face-to-face learning modality. Nursery 1 began to be offered for three-year-old learners, and Nursery 2 for four-year- old learners. DepEd approved the SHS permit for General Academic Strand (GAS) and Technical-Vocational-Livelihood-Home Economics (TVL-HE) Strand towards the end of this school year. Facilities and learning equipment were put up: the school canteen, built during the pandemic years, was fully utilized at the beginning of this school year, TVL building was constructed towards the end of this school year; white boards and smart TVs were installed.

**Our Mission:** To empower every student to achieve personal and academic excellence, become lifelong learners, and responsible, compassionate global citizens.

Student Services

Student services are essential supplements and tools for the realization of the Institution's objectives. Thus, OLAS Basic Education Department provides:

1. GUIDANCE CENTER

Provides the pupils efficient guidance, counseling, testing, refer- ral services, intervention program, other counseling related services. This is programmed med by the Guidance Counselor in cooperation with teacher counselors or class advisers and other outside resource per- sons. All students should feel free to see the counselor.

2. MEDICAL CLINIC

Provides services for the maintenance of health and sanitation which includes diagnosis, necessary emergency treatments, and immun- ization prescribed by the Department of Health. A doctor and a nurse are in-charge of attending to medical services.

3. LIBRARIES

Provides up-to-date educational materials, books, and references. All students are encouraged to do supplementary reading and use the ma- terials available and not just take their rest or do their homework in the library. The general library rules should be observed. All other rules are posted in the library and enforced by the librarian who also takes charge in teaching the students how to make use of the materials.

4. BOOKSTORE

Provides textbooks and school supplies for the students at current prices. Purchases should be made at the proper time.

5. SCHOOL CANTEEN

This service is provided to keep the pupils within the school premises during recess time. Its main concern is fair price and sanita- tion. Purchases should be made at proper times, namely, recess and lunch breaks.

6. GUARD POST

One of the most important concerns of the school is the security of students while in the school premise. The guard's main duty is to pre- vent unwanted elements or intruders from entering the campus. Hence, he is responsible for the safety of all properties and person within the school compound.

7. CO-CURRICULAR

Clubs, intramurals, inter-school meets, social actions, field trips, retreats, recollections are activities encouraged and calendared by the school for their educational, spiritual, and social values to provide the students practical application of classroom learning; to acquire the spirit of positive competition, sportsmanship, camaraderie, and closer relation- ship with God and other people.
